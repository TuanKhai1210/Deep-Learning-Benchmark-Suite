    <span class="eyebrow">01 / PROBLEM STATEMENT</span>
    <h2>Underwater Semantic Segmentation: Materials, Marine Life, and Hardware</h2>
    <p>Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) inspecting seafloors face extreme optical degradation: severe wavelength-dependent light attenuation, turbidity, backscatter, artificial spotlight vignetting, and pervasive biofouling. Marine debris is particularly difficult to distinguish from benthic fauna and sediment because object geometry, degradation state, scale, and illumination vary continuously across dive sites.</p>
    
    <p>The objective of this project is to perform <strong>dense pixel-level semantic segmentation</strong> of marine debris, biological organisms, and robotic equipment in seafloor imagery. Formally, given an underwater RGB image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, a model predicts a dense categorical label map $\widehat{y} \in \{0, \dots, 16\}^{H \times W}$. There are <strong>17 mutually exclusive semantic classes: background (class 0) plus 16 foreground categories</strong>:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Robotic Equipment:</strong> ROV body and manipulator arms (class 1).</li>
      <li><strong>Marine Flora &amp; Fauna:</strong> Sea plants (class 2) and six animal categories (classes 3–8: fish, starfish, shells, crab, eel, etc.).</li>
      <li><strong>Marine Debris Materials:</strong> Eight trash material categories (classes 9–16: trash_etc, fabric, fishing gear, metal, paper, plastic, rubber, wood).</li>
      <li><strong>Ignored Regions:</strong> Ambiguous polygon overlap boundaries and letterbox padding pixels receive ignore label <code>255</code> and are excluded from both supervision and evaluation.</li>
    </ul>

    <p>This benchmark was designed around three central research questions:</p>

    <div class="highlight-stat-row">
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q1</div>
        <div class="label">Architectural Capacity</div>
        <div class="desc">Under a strictly frozen video-disjoint split, how do a scratch convolutional U-Net and a pretrained hierarchical vision transformer (SegFormer-B0) compare on foreground and trash overlap?</div>
      </div>
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q2</div>
        <div class="label">Color Augmentation</div>
        <div class="desc">With architecture and starting weights matched by seed, does mild training RGB color jitter improve trash overlap while preserving foreground quality under the pre-declared proposal threshold?</div>
      </div>
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q3</div>
        <div class="label">Error Taxonomy &amp; Cost</div>
        <div class="desc">What error modes persist (foreground omission, camouflage, material confusion), and what does each model cost to train and run under a matched Tesla T4 forward latency protocol?</div>
      </div>

    <h2>TrashCan 1.0 Material: Imagery, Annotation QA, and Class Support</h2>
    <p>TrashCan 1.0 (Hong, Fulton, &amp; Sattar, 2020) provides annotated seafloor imagery sourced from the Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology (JAMSTEC). The <em>Material</em> variant supplies object-level COCO polygons detailing what materials debris consists of (plastic, metal, wood, rubber, fabric, fishing gear, paper, etc.) rather than generic trash bounding boxes.</p>

    <p>Our exploratory data analysis (EDA), audited on 5 October 2026, confirmed:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>7,212 readable RGB images:</strong> Comprising 6,008 original training images and 1,204 original validation images across 312 filename-derived video sequence groups.</li>
      <li><strong>12,336 object polygon annotations:</strong> 9,741 training and 2,595 validation annotations across 16 foreground classes. <strong>Zero</strong> empty rasterized annotations were found under Pillow 12.3.0.</li>
      <li><strong>Resolution distribution:</strong> 3,967 images ($55.01\%$) at $480 \times 270$ resolution (16:9 widescreen) and 3,245 images ($44.99\%$) at $480 \times 360$ resolution (4:3 standard), totaling 1,074,859,200 native pixels.</li>
      <li><strong>Extreme class imbalance:</strong> <code>trash_plastic</code> encompasses 1,864 annotations ($17.88$ million supervised pixels, $1.66\%$ of dataset), whereas rare classes such as <code>trash_rubber</code> contain only 142 annotations ($0.49$ million pixels, $0.046\%$ of dataset across 7 video groups) and <code>animal_shells</code> contain 249 annotations ($0.20$ million pixels).</li>
      <li><strong>Background dominance:</strong> Background pixels account for $89.35\%$ ($960.34$ million pixels) of the native canvas, supervised foreground pixels account for $10.61\%$ ($114.09$ million pixels), and conflicting foreground overlap pixels account for $0.041\%$ ($436,844$ pixels).</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Aspect Ratio Distribution &amp; Letterbox Engineering</h3>
    <p>Marine debris objects exhibit vital geometric features that indicate material type (e.g., circularity of aluminum beverage cans, thin elongation of plastic ropes/fishing gear, cylindrical profile of glass or plastic bottles). The dataset comprises two native resolutions:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>$480 \times 270$ (16:9 Aspect Ratio):</strong> 3,967 images ($55.01\%$)</li>
      <li><strong>$480 \times 360$ (4:3 Aspect Ratio):</strong> 3,245 images ($44.99\%$)</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Direct resizing via standard interpolation distorts physical aspect ratios, compressing or stretching debris geometry and corrupting material cues. We implement <strong>aspect-ratio-preserving letterboxing to a uniform $512 \times 512$ canvas</strong>: RGB frames are scaled using bilinear interpolation with symmetric black $[0,0,0]$ padding; target masks are scaled using nearest-neighbor interpolation with ignore label <code>255</code> padding. This guarantees zero distortion of physical proportions, and padded pixels contribute zero loss during backpropagation and zero weight in evaluation metrics.</p>

    <span class="eyebrow">03 / LEAKAGE DEFENSE</span>
    <h2>The Video-Disjoint Principle: Zero Video-Group Overlap</h2>
    <p>A critical discovery during our preliminary audit on 5 October 2026 was that <strong>the original TrashCan dataset split shared 127 video sequence groups between training (6,008 images) and validation (1,204 images)</strong>. Because underwater video frames recorded seconds apart are visually near-identical, evaluating models on the original split leads to extreme data leakage and deceptively inflated validation scores.</p>

    <h3>The Video-Disjoint Partition (Split Seed 36)</h3>
    <p>To establish a rigorous, realistic benchmark, we constructed a <strong>video-disjoint partition using split seed 36</strong>. Images were clustered by their underlying video sequence prefix (extracted from the <code>vid_*</code> filename token before <code>_frame</code>) into 312 disjoint video groups. A deterministic greedy local search over 24 candidate assignments targeted approximately a 70/15/15 image count split while enforcing two strict invariants:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Zero pairwise video overlap:</strong> No video group appears in more than one partition ($\text{Train} \cap \text{Val} = \emptyset$, $\text{Train} \cap \text{Test} = \emptyset$, $\text{Val} \cap \text{Test} = \emptyset$).</li>
      <li><strong>Class presence guarantee:</strong> Every single foreground class is supported by at least 3 training video groups and at least 2 video groups in each held-out partition (validation and test).</li>
    </ul>

  <section id="methodology" class="study-section">
    <span class="eyebrow">04 / METHODOLOGY</span>
    <h2>Architectures, Objective Formulation, and Controlled Experiment</h2>
    <p>The segmentation pipeline letterboxes all inputs to a uniform $512 \times 512$ resolution while preserving native aspect ratios. RGB scaling uses bilinear interpolation, mask scaling uses nearest-neighbor, letterbox padding is set to black $[0,0,0]$ on RGB, and target padding is set to ignore label <code>255</code>. Tensors are normalized using standard ImageNet statistics.</p>

    <div class="study-two-col">
      <div>
        <h3>1. Scratch U-Net Baseline</h3>
        <p>A compact convolutional encoder-decoder with skip connections, initialized entirely from scratch (Ronneberger et al., 2015):</p>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Encoder:</strong> 4 downsampling stages with channel widths 16, 32, 64, 128, 256. Each block contains two bias-free $3 \times 3$ convolutions, Group Normalization (8 groups), and ReLU. Downsampling uses $2 \times 2$ max pooling.</li>
          <li><strong>Decoder:</strong> Bilinear upsampling ($2\times$) with feature concatenation from corresponding encoder skip connections, followed by a double-convolution block.</li>
          <li><strong>Head:</strong> $1 \times 1$ convolution producing 17 logits per pixel.</li>
          <li><strong>Parameters:</strong> 1,964,369 (100% trainable, zero frozen).</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>2. Pretrained SegFormer-B0</h3>
        <p>A hierarchical vision transformer encoder paired with an all-MLP decoder (Xie et al., 2021):</p>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Encoder:</strong> Mix Transformer (MiT-B0) with overlapped patch merging, efficient self-attention, and mix-FFN layers. Initialized from <code>nvidia/segformer-b0-finetuned-ade-512-512</code> (commit <code>489d5cd8</code>).</li>
          <li><strong>Decoder:</strong> Lightweight all-MLP decoder aggregating multi-scale features from 4 encoder stages (widths 32, 64, 160, 256).</li>
          <li><strong>Head:</strong> The original 150-class ADE20K classifier is replaced with a newly initialized 17-class classifier.</li>
          <li><strong>Parameters:</strong> 3,718,513 (100% trainable, end-to-end fine-tuning).</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h3>Loss Function: Cross-Entropy + Foreground Soft Dice</h3>
    <p>To combat extreme background dominance, models are optimized using an unweighted sum of multiclass cross-entropy and foreground soft Dice loss:</p>
    $$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \frac{1}{|C_B|} \sum_{c \in C_B} \left(1 - \frac{2 \sum_{i \in V_B} p_{ic} y_{ic} + \epsilon}{\sum_{i \in V_B} (p_{ic} + y_{ic}) + \epsilon}\right)$$
    <p>Where $V_B$ is the set of valid pixels in batch $B$ (excluding ignore label 255), $C_B$ is the set of foreground classes present in the batch ground-truth, $p_{ic}$ is the softmax probability, $y_{ic}$ is the binary ground-truth indicator, and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. Batches lacking foreground contribute zero soft Dice loss.</p>

    <h3>Controlled Experiment: Color Augmentation (S0 vs. S1)</h3>
    <p>Underwater lighting causes chromatic distortion and blue/green color cast. To isolate the effect of color variation on generalization to unseen videos, we formulated a controlled comparison:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>S0 (Color Off):</strong> Deterministic RGB letterboxing and ImageNet normalization without color modification.</li>
      <li><strong>S1 (Color On):</strong> Training-only random color jitter applied with probability $p=0.5$. Brightness, contrast, and saturation factors are randomly sampled in $[0.9, 1.1]$, and hue shift in $[-0.01, 0.01]$ with randomized operation ordering. Ground-truth masks remain unmodified.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Within each seed, S0 and S1 share identical initial parameter weights, dataset shuffle order, learning rate, loss function, and early stopping rules.</p>

  <section id="limitations" class="study-section">
    <span class="eyebrow">09 / LIMITATIONS &amp; CONCLUSION</span>
    <h2>Honest Scientific Boundaries and Synthesis</h2>
    
    <div class="study-two-col">
      <div>
        <h3>Methodological Limitations</h3>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Dive-Level Scene Correlation:</strong> While video-disjoint partitioning prevents consecutive-frame leakage, different video clips recorded during the same research dive may share water turbidity, sediment hue, and lighting conditions.</li>
          <li><strong>Extreme Class Support Imbalance:</strong> Rare categories (<code>trash_rubber</code> with 142 annotations, <code>animal_shells</code> with 249 annotations) have very few held-out test videos, resulting in high sample variance.</li>
          <li><strong>Cross-Platform Continuation:</strong> S0 at seed 67 resumed on Colab after 7 Kaggle epochs due to session limits. While RNG and optimizer states were preserved bit-for-bit, exact cross-runtime reproducibility requires identical hardware.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Core Answers to Research Questions</h3>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Q1 Capacity &amp; Transfer:</strong> SegFormer-B0 decisively outperforms scratch U-Net (+11.34 pp FG mIoU, +4.81 pp Trash mIoU), demonstrating the critical value of ADE20K pretraining and hierarchical attention for underwater scenes.</li>
          <li><strong>Q2 Color Augmentation:</strong> Mild training RGB color jitter does <em>not</em> provide a consistent benefit across seeds; it met the proposal rule only on seed 69 and increased trash-to-background omission rates on all three seeds.</li>
          <li><strong>Q3 Trade-offs &amp; Efficiency:</strong> U-Net is 23.5% faster during forward inference (21.24 ms vs. 27.74 ms/img), but its 51.0% foreground omission rate makes it unsuitable for reliable debris localization.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
