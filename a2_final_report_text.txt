Abstract
We compare scratch U-Net and two pretrained SegFormer-B0 configurations on a video-
disjoint TrashCan-Material split. The SegFormer configurations differ in training color
augmentation. We report nine runs over three seeds on the same 1,078 held-out images.
Mean test foreground mean intersection over union (mIoU) is 4.52% for U-Net, 15.86% for
SegFormer without augmentation and 15.41% for SegFormer with augmentation. Both
SegFormerconfigurations exceed U-Net ateach measured seed. Theaugmentation difference
changes sign across seeds. The augmented model assigns more true trash pixels to
background at each seed. Matched qualitative cases show thin-object recovery, foreground
omissions and material confusion.
We report inference cost under one T4 batch protocol and keep the seed-level outcomes
visible. The results describe one fixed split and recorded Kaggle and Colab execution
profiles.
Keywords:semantic segmentation, marine debris, TrashCan, U-Net, SegFormer, video-
disjoint evaluation.

1 Problem and data description
1.1 Task and research objectives
Underwater debris is difficult to distinguish from marine life and the seafloor because
object appearance, scale, illumination and occlusion vary. TrashCan supplies underwater
images with object-level annotations for marine debris research[1]. This project converts
the polygons into a semantic segmentation task: each valid image pixel receives one class
label, without distinguishing separate instances of the same class.
For an RGB imagex∈R H×W×3, the model predicts a mapˆy∈ {0, . . . ,16}H×W. There
are17 output classes: background plus 16 foreground categories. The foreground
comprises ROV, plant, six animal categories and eight trash categories. Ignore label 255 is
excluded from supervision and evaluation.
An underwater image may contain several objects and classes. The task requires the model
to locate their boundaries and assign a category at each pixel. Two pieces of plastic receive
the same class ID. The remotely operated vehicle (ROV) and marine life are foreground
categories alongside trash. Padding and ambiguous cross-class overlap are excluded through
the ignore label.
The project asks three questions.
1. Under a fixed video-disjoint split, how do a scratch U-Net and a pretrained SegFormer-
B0 compare on foreground and trash overlap? The comparison includes each configu-
ration’s architecture, initialization and learning rate.
2. With the SegFormer architecture and starting weights matched by seed, does mild
training RGB color jitter improve trash overlap while preserving foreground quality?
The proposal criterion is at least a 1-point gain in validation trash mIoU, with at most
a 1-point loss in each of validation foreground mIoU and Dice.
3. What types of errors persist, and what does each configuration cost to run? We
use pixel-level class confusions, aligned test predictions and recorded forward latency
under one matched Tesla T4 protocol.
The practical work covers polygon-to-mask conversion, splitting by video, a shared training
and evaluation engine, nine model–seed runs, and verification of saved results. The criterion
in Question 2 is a predeclared validation target. The held-out evaluation tests the selected
checkpoints independently of that criterion.

1.4 Mask conversion, preprocessing and checks
Polygons are rasterized on the native image canvas with Pillow 12.3.0. Same-class polygons
are unioned; pixels where different foreground classes overlap receive ignore 255. Out-of-
canvas regions are clipped. The EDA found no annotation that rasterized to an empty
mask under this converter.
Each image is letterboxed to512× 512while preserving aspect ratio. RGB uses bilinear
resizing, masks use nearest-neighbor resizing, RGB padding is black and target padding is
ignore 255. The tensor is normalized with ImageNet channel means(0.485, 0.456, 0.406)
and standard deviations(0.229, 0.224, 0.225). There is no label reduction or second image-
processor resize.
The training adapter checks the published manifest digest, source annotation hashes, image
dimensions, membership and native target hash. The supplied checks cover 5,057 training
and 1,077 validation images. Figure 1.3 reproduces existing EDA annotation checks; it is
not a gallery of model predictions. At evaluation resolution, ignored pixels include the much
larger letterbox padding region as well as native annotation conflicts. For example, U-Net
test ignore fraction is 34.06%; this does not mean that 34.06% of the native annotations
are conflicting.

2.2 Segmentation theory and tensor interface
Each model mapsB× 3 ×H×W RGB inputs to alignedB× 17 ×H×W logits. A logit
is an unnormalized class score. The softmax across the 17 channels gives a probability
distribution at each location, and the largest logit determines the discrete predicted label.
In this experimentH = W = 512. The mask tensor contains integer labels at the same
spatial coordinates. Pixels with label 255 contribute to neither loss nor the confusion
matrix.
A small piece of debris can occupy only a few pixels while most of the image shows the
seafloor. Strided encoding increases the context available to classify the small region
but reduces its spatial resolution. U-Net restores detail through high-resolution encoder
skips. SegFormer fuses four feature scales that capture different amounts of context. Both
decoders align their predictions to the input canvas.

2.3 Scratch U-Net baseline
The compact U-Net follows an encoder–decoder design with skip connections[3]. The
implemented version has four downsampling stages and channel widths 16,32,64,128,256.
Each block contains two bias-free3× 3convolutions, eight-group Group Normalization
and ReLU. Downsampling uses2× 2max pooling. The decoder upsamples bilinearly with
align_corners=false, concatenates encoder features, and applies another two-convolution
block. A1×1head produces 17 logits per pixel.
All 1,964,369 parameter elements are trainable, with zero frozen parameters. The model is
initialized from scratch. The scratch configuration supplies the architecture baseline.
At each decoder level, the upsampled feature and the same-resolution encoder feature are
concatenated channel-wise. The encoder path therefore carries local texture and boundaries
around the low-resolution bottleneck. The stages of the implemented network are shown
in Figure 2.2.
2.4 Pretrained SegFormer-B0 and full fine-tuning
SegFormer combines a hierarchical Transformer encoder with a lightweight multilayer-
perceptron decoder[4]. The implementation loads nvidia/segformer-b0-finetuned
-ade-512-512 , pinned to revision 489d5cd81a0b59fab9b7ea758d3548ebe99677da .
The pretrained encoder and compatible decoder weights are retained, while the 150-class
ADE20K classifier is replaced by a newly initialized 17-class classifier.
The complete model is fine-tuned end to end: encoder, decoder and classifier parameters
all receive updates. Total and trainable counts are both 3,718,513; frozen count is zero.
Pretrained initialization therefore does not imply a frozen backbone. Lower-resolution
raw logits are bilinearly interpolated to the 512-canvas target before computing loss and
argmax predictions.
The main-run initial-state hashes match between S0 and S1 within each of the three
matched run seeds.
The Mix Transformer B0 encoder uses overlapping patch projections at successive spatial
strides and outputs four feature maps at resolutions1/4,1 /8,1 /16and1 /32of the input.
Self-attention mixes information between locations. Spatial reduction of keys and values
reduces its cost at the larger resolutions. The decoder projects each scale to width 256,
upsamples to the1/4grid, concatenates and fuses the features, then applies the 17-class
head. The pinned architecture configuration specifies channel widths 32, 64, 160 and 256
and two blocks per stage[5].

with coefficient 1:

Here VB is the set of valid pixels in a batch,CB contains foreground classes present in
the target, pic is the softmax probability,yic is the one-hot target andϵ = 10−6. A batch
with no foreground contributes zero Dice loss. The optimizer skips all-ignore batches. The
logged epoch loss averages batch objectives weighted by valid pixel count.
Cross-entropy penalizes low probability on the correct class at each valid pixel. Soft Dice
gives equal weight to each present foreground class. A model can reduce cross-entropy
across a large seafloor region while still missing a small piece of debris. Dice penalizes that
omission for the debris class.
For hard predictions, the evaluator accumulates one 17-by 17 confusion matrix across the
whole partition. For classc,

accuracy is the diagonal sum divided by valid pixels.

2.6 Training budget, checkpointing and environment
Both model families use AdamW, weight decay 0.01, batch 4, up to 40 epochs, patience
8, FP 32, no learning-rate scheduler and no mixed precision. U-Net learning rate is10−3;
SegFormer uses6 × 10−5. Training seeds 69420, 67 and 69 are distinct from split seed
36. The training loader shuffles with a separate generator seeded by run seed plus epoch;
validation/test are unshuffled.
The best checkpoint maximizes validation foreground mIoU; a tie uses lower validation loss,
then the earlier epoch. Each completed epoch saves model, AdamW state, random-number
states, history, best selection and early-stopping counter. Resume restores this state; an
incomplete epoch is repeated. The U-Net continuation belongs to the same experiment
and is not an independent seed.
The supplied runs/pilots record a Tesla T4 with 14.56 GiB memory, Python 3.13.16,
PyTorch 2.11.0+cu 130, torchvision 0.26.0+cu 130 and NumPy 2.1.3. Pillow 12.3.0 and
Transformers 4.57.6 are pinned. TF 32 is disabled and CUDA deterministic algorithms use
warning-only mode for the bilinear-backward limitation. These settings and saved RNG
state do not promise cross-runtime GPU bitwise identity.
2.7 Color augmentation and execution profiles
S0 disables color jitter. S1 applies it to training RGB with probability 0.5: brightness,
contrast and saturation factors are sampled in[0.9, 1.1], hue shift in[−0.01, 0.01], with
shuffled operation order. Masks are unchanged. A seed/epoch/image-specific random
stream keeps augmentation separate from model and sampling randomness. There is no
geometric augmentation; held-out images are unaugmented.
The paired configurations share architecture, pretrained revision, seed, split, normalization,
optimizer, learning rate, loss and budget. The intended treatment is the training RGB
color switch. Table 3.1 records actual execution profiles. S0 at seed 67 resumed on Colab
from a Kaggle checkpoint after seven epochs. This continuation preserves the seed and
training history.
Underwater lighting and camera conditions can change apparent color and contrast. The
experiment tests whether mild photometric variation helps robustness while preserving the
spatial target. Color can also help distinguish materials, so the effect on trash categories
is empirical. Figure 1.4 shows the actual transform branches. The S0/S1 comparison
controls the initial state and nominal training protocol. The U/S0 comparison also changes
initialization, parameter count and learning rate.

4 Limitations and conclusion
The held-out evaluation uses one fixed split based on source video identifiers. Independence
across dives and locations remains unverified. Rare classes and ignored cross-class conflicts
limit what can be inferred from pixel overlap. U-Net is the smallest configuration and
has the lowest observed forward cost at seed 69. Both SegFormer configurations give
higher foreground and trash overlap in the measured tests. The selected augmentation
does not show a consistent benefit across the three pairs. Further repeats in one execution
environment and a test set from independent dives would better separate these effects.
