  <section id="problem" class="study-section">
    <span class="eyebrow">01 / PROBLEM STATEMENT</span>
    <h2>How Does Architecture Shape Representation and Generalization?</h2>
    <p>Given a low-resolution grayscale clothing image $X_i \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 28 \times 28}$, the classification task predicts one of ten categorical garment labels $y_i \in \{0, \dots, 9\}$. A parameterized classifier $f_\theta$ produces ten unnormalized real-valued logits $z_i = f_\theta(X_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$, yielding the predicted class $\widehat{y}_i = \arg\max_{k \in \{0,\dots,9\}} z_{i,k}$. The objective function during training is sample-weighted multiclass cross-entropy.</p>
    
    <p>While deep learning benchmarks frequently rank models solely by raw top-1 accuracy, such scalar summaries conceal critical trade-offs between inductive biases, parameter counts, and deployment latencies. This benchmark was designed around four specific research questions:</p>
    
    <div class="highlight-stat-row">
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q1</div>
        <div class="label">Predictive Quality</div>
        <div class="desc">How do Linear, MLP, Custom CNN, GRU, and Transformer architectures compare in held-out test accuracy and macro-F1 when trained under identical conditions?</div>
      </div>
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q2</div>
        <div class="label">Representation Bias</div>
        <div class="desc">How do flattened pixel vectors, 2D spatial feature maps, row-sequence timesteps, and patch tokens relate to class-specific error patterns and apparel confusions?</div>
      </div>
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q3</div>
        <div class="label">Efficiency &amp; Compute</div>
        <div class="desc">What quantitative trade-offs arise among model parameter footprints, GPU training-loop durations, and dedicated CPU inference latencies?</div>
      </div>
      <div class="highlight-stat-card">
        <div class="num">Q4</div>
        <div class="label">Seed Stability</div>
        <div class="desc">How sensitive are convergence trajectories, metric variances, and ranking stability across distinct random initializations (seeds 69420, 67, and 69)?</div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

Unlike digits, clothing items share silhouette outlines, folds, and neckline subtleties, requiring models to learn discriminative local textures and spatial relationships.</p>

    <h3>Preprocessing, Normalization &amp; Leakage Defense</h3>
    <p>All models operate on identical inputs: <code>float32</code> tensors of shape <code>[B, 1, 28, 28]</code>. Data transformations follow strict leakage controls:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Training-Only Augmentation:</strong> Random crop yielding $28 \times 28$ output after 2 pixels of constant zero padding on each side. Horizontal flipping was intentionally disabled to preserve asymmetrical fashion details.</li>
      <li><strong>Isolated Normalization:</strong> Pixel scaling to $[0, 1]$, followed by channel standardization:
        $$x' = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}, \quad \text{with } \mu = 0.28585961, \quad \sigma = 0.35279038$$
        Crucially, $\mu$ and $\sigma$ were fitted strictly on the unaugmented training split; validation and test sets were standardized using these frozen training statistics without contributing to them.</li>
      <li><strong>Deterministic Evaluation:</strong> Validation and test evaluation pipelines use deterministic tensor conversion and normalization with zero random jitter.</li>
    </ul>

    <!-- Mathematical Architecture Details -->
    <h3>Architectural Formulations</h3>
    <details class="model-detail" open>
      <summary><span><span class="eyebrow">MODEL 01</span> Linear / Softmax Baseline</span></summary>
      <p>The linear baseline maps the flattened 784-dimensional image directly to 10 class logits via an affine transformation:
      $$z = W \cdot \operatorname{flatten}(X) + b, \quad W \in \mathbb{R}^{10 \times 784}, \quad b \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$$
      Parameters: $784 \times 10 + 10 = 7,850$. This baseline exposes the performance limit of linear decision boundaries in raw pixel space.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="model-detail">
      <summary><span><span class="eyebrow">MODEL 02</span> Deep Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)</span></summary>
      <p>The MLP introduces nonlinear representation capacity through two hidden layers with ReLU activations and dropout:
      $$h_1 = \operatorname{Dropout}_{0.1}(\operatorname{ReLU}(W_1 x + b_1)), \quad h_2 = \operatorname{Dropout}_{0.1}(\operatorname{ReLU}(W_2 h_1 + b_2)), \quad z = W_3 h_2 + b_3$$
      With dimensions $784 \to 256 \to 128 \to 10$, total parameters equal $(784 \times 256 + 256) + (256 \times 128 + 128) + (128 \times 10 + 10) = 235,146$. While expressive, it discards spatial adjacency.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="model-detail">
      <summary><span><span class="eyebrow">MODEL 03</span> Custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)</span></summary>
      <p>The custom CNN enforces translation equivariance and spatial hierarchy through local receptive fields:
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Conv Block 1:</strong> $\text{Conv2d}(1 \to 32, k=3, p=1) \to \text{ReLU} \to \text{MaxPool2d}(2, 2)$ &rarr; shape $[B, 32, 14, 14]$</li>
        <li><strong>Conv Block 2:</strong> $\text{Conv2d}(32 \to 64, k=3, p=1) \to \text{ReLU} \to \text{MaxPool2d}(2, 2)$ &rarr; shape $[B, 64, 7, 7]$</li>
        <li><strong>Classifier Head:</strong> $\text{AdaptiveAvgPool2d}(1, 1) \to \text{Flatten} \to \text{Linear}(64 \to 128) \to \text{ReLU} \to \text{Dropout}_{0.1} \to \text{Linear}(128 \to 10)$</li>
      </ul>
      Total parameters: 28,426 (87.9% smaller than MLP).</p>
    </details>

    <details class="model-detail">
      <summary><span><span class="eyebrow">MODEL 04</span> Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) Classifier</span></summary>
      <p>Treats the $28 \times 28$ image as a 28-timestep sequence of row vectors $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{28}$ ($t = 1, \dots, 28$). A unidirectional GRU with hidden size 128 aggregates top-to-bottom scanlines:
      $$r_t = \sigma(W_{ir} x_t + b_{ir} + W_{hr} h_{t-1} + b_{hr}), \quad z_t = \sigma(W_{iz} x_t + b_{iz} + W_{hz} h_{t-1} + b_{hz})$$
      $$n_t = \tanh(W_{in} x_t + b_{in} + r_t \odot (W_{hn} h_{t-1} + b_{hn})), \quad h_t = (1 - z_t) \odot n_t + z_t \odot h_{t-1}$$
      The final state $h_{28}$ is projected via $\text{Linear}(128 \to 10)$ to produce logits. Parameters: $60,672 \text{ (cell)} + 1,290 \text{ (head)} = 61,962$.</p>
    </details>

    <details class="model-detail">
      <summary><span><span class="eyebrow">MODEL 05</span> Vision Transformer (ViT) Classifier</span></summary>
      <p>The image is decomposed into a $7 \times 7$ grid of non-overlapping $4 \times 4$ patches. Each 16-pixel patch is linearly projected to $d=64$, followed by learned 1D positional embeddings:
      $$P \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 49 \times 16}, \quad H_0 = P W_E + b_E + E_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 49 \times 64}$$
      The sequence passes through 2 pre-LayerNorm Transformer encoder blocks with 4 attention heads ($d_h = 16$) and MLP expansion factor 4 ($64 \to 256 \to 64$ with GELU):
      $$U_\ell = H_{\ell-1} + \operatorname{Dropout}_{0.1}(\operatorname{MHSA}(\operatorname{LN}(H_{\ell-1}))), \quad H_\ell = U_\ell + \operatorname{Dropout}_{0.1}(\operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{LN}(U_\ell)))$$
      Final classification applies mean pooling over all 49 token embeddings followed by a linear projection $\text{Linear}(64 \to 10)$. Parameters: 104,970.</p>
    </details>
  </section>

  <!-- 7. COMPARISON AND DISCUSSION -->
  <section id="comparison" class="study-section">
    <span class="eyebrow">06 / COMPARISON AND DISCUSSION</span>
    <h2>Inductive Biases, Accuracy vs. Footprint, and Runtime Trade-offs</h2>

    <figure class="report-figure">
      <img src="{{ '/assets/a1/comparison.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Comparative official test accuracy and macro-F1" />
      <figcaption><strong>Figure 9:</strong> Official test accuracy and macro-F1 across all five architectures (mean &plusmn; sample SD, $n=3$ seeds). Both axes retain the 0–100% scale.</figcaption>
    </figure>

    <div class="study-two-col">
      <div>
        <h3>1. Predictive Accuracy vs. Parameter Footprint</h3>
        <p>The <strong>Custom Transformer</strong> achieves the highest test accuracy (<strong>91.267%</strong>) and macro-F1 (<strong>91.234%</strong>), demonstrating the efficacy of global self-attention over image patches. However, the <strong>Custom CNN</strong> delivers nearly identical predictive capability (<strong>90.690%</strong> test accuracy, a difference of just 0.577 percentage points) while requiring <strong>72.9% fewer parameters</strong> (28,426 vs. 104,970).</p>
        <p>Conversely, the <strong>MLP</strong> holds the largest parameter footprint (235,146 weights)—over 8 times larger than the CNN—yet trails the CNN by nearly a full percentage point (89.720%), proving that raw parameter count without structural inductive bias leads to suboptimal representation.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>2. Spatial Locality vs. Sequential Scanlines</h3>
        <p>The CNN's inductive bias (local weight sharing and spatial subsampling) precisely matches 2D visual structures. The <strong>GRU</strong>, forced to treat 2D images as an artificial sequence of 28 rows, achieves a surprisingly respectable <strong>90.297%</strong> test accuracy. However, its sequential recurrence prevents parallelization across spatial dimensions during inference, resulting in lower CPU throughput (9,776 img/s) than the MLP.</p>
        <p>The Linear baseline (78.377%) establishes that linear hyperplanes cannot disentangle intricate garment folds without hierarchical feature extraction.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- 9. LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION -->
  <section id="limitations" class="study-section">
    <span class="eyebrow">08 / LIMITATIONS &amp; CONCLUSION</span>
    <h2>Honest Scientific Boundaries and Synthesis</h2>
    
    <div class="study-two-col">
      <div>
        <h3>Methodological Limitations</h3>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Domain &amp; Resolution:</strong> Fashion-MNIST's $28 \times 28$ grayscale format lacks background clutter, scale variation, and color channels present in real-world computer vision tasks.</li>
          <li><strong>Sample Size Across Seeds:</strong> Three training seeds provide an initial variance estimate but are insufficient for formal statistical significance hypothesis tests (e.g., paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests).</li>
          <li><strong>Heterogeneous Environments:</strong> Colab T4 GPU loop durations and local Windows CPU inference times cannot be directly combined into an end-to-end latency metric.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Benchmark Conclusions</h3>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>Q1 Quality:</strong> The Vision Transformer leads overall held-out accuracy (91.267%) and macro-F1 (91.234%), closely followed by Custom CNN (90.690%).</li>
          <li><strong>Q2 Representation:</strong> 2D local spatial maps and patch tokens effectively resolve garment silhouettes; raw pixel vectors fail on overlapping apparel cuts.</li>
          <li><strong>Q3 Efficiency:</strong> CNN is the clear engineering sweet spot, offering near-SOTA accuracy with 72.9% fewer parameters and lower training cost than the Transformer.</li>
          <li><strong>Q4 Stability:</strong> Standard deviation across seeds was small (&le; 0.324%), confirming that ranking differences are stable.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
